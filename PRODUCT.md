# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Product designers, UX practitioners, design engineers, and product teams working through complex interface problems. They use Cognitive Engine when they need to move from an observed behavior such as abandonment, alert fatigue, low trust, or errors into a concrete design intervention and AI-ready prompt.

## Product Purpose

Cognitive Engine turns UX problems into actionable design instructions using cognitive science, behavioral science, and HCI principles. Success means a user can describe a messy interface problem, understand why specific principles apply, see practical actions and anti-patterns, then generate a structured prompt for tools such as Figma Make, Windsurf, ChatGPT, Claude, Linear, or Notion.

## Brand Personality

Evidence-led, practical, calm. The product should feel like a sharp design partner: clear enough for fast decisions, rigorous enough to trust, and restrained enough to keep attention on the user’s workflow.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic AI prompt-library pages, decorative SaaS dashboards, vague principle encyclopedias, over-animated “AI magic” effects, and interfaces that teach theory without showing what to build. Avoid noisy gradients, novelty controls, and any visual system that makes cognitive load worse while claiming to reduce it.

## Design Principles

1. Show the reasoning: every recommendation should explain why it matched.
2. Move from theory to action: principles should become patterns, constraints, and examples.
3. Reduce cognitive load in the product itself: forms, filters, and prompts should be easy to scan and recover from.
4. Keep standard controls familiar: use polished product UI patterns over novelty.
5. Make outputs portable: generated prompts should be structured, copyable, and tailored to the selected tool.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. Maintain visible focus states, readable contrast, keyboard-operable controls, touch targets of at least 44px where practical, and reduced-motion fallbacks for animation. The interface should support users under time pressure, high cognitive load, or low confidence in design theory.
