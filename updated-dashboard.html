Recommended Principles

Based on "Speed up decisions" in "Decision & approvals"

Hick’s Law Optimization

High Reduction

Minimize the number of stimuli to reduce time and errors in decision-making processes.

Why it applies

Decision nodes currently present 12+ parallel options without hierarchy.

Impact Type

Latency Reduction

construction Design Actions

  • • Categorize primary vs secondary actions.
  • • Implement progressive disclosure for 'Edge Case' data.
  • • Group related laboratory variables under semantic headers.

info Usage Rules

WHEN TO USE

During high-speed diagnostic triage where reaction time is critical.

WHEN NOT TO USE

In investigative deep-dives where data synthesis requires all raw points visible.

biotech Life Sciences Example

Redesigned the Gene Sequencing analyzer to hide 'Low Confidence' alleles until explicitly requested by the pathologist.

Von Restorff Effect

Moderate Reduction

Predictably isolate the most important information to ensure it is the first thing noticed.

Why it applies

Critical patient alerts are visually identical to routine status notifications.

keyboard_arrow_down

Miller’s Law Chunking

High Reduction

Break down complex datasets into smaller, digestible 'chunks' of 5–9 items.

Why it applies

Form sections contain 40+ fields in a single vertical scrolling stack.

keyboard_arrow_down

Recognition Over Recall

High Reduction

Minimize the user's memory load by making objects, actions, and options visible.

Why it applies

System requires memorization of keyboard shortcuts for common actions.
